About

Who is behind Zombie Facts, what we claim and what we don't, and what happens when we get something wrong.

Who runs it

Zombie Facts is an independent project run by Colin Robinson. It has no staff, no sponsor and no political or commercial backer. The only running cost is the domain name, paid out of pocket. There are no ads, no trackers that follow you around the web, and nothing to buy.

On Wikipedia, our notes to editors come from the account XavierCatastrophe, and every note says it was found with this tracker. We don't edit articles with automated tools, and we don't add links to this site in Wikipedia articles.

What a case means

A case says one checkable thing: a source was retracted (or a claim was rated false by a fact-checker), and a page still uses it without telling readers. Every case links to the official retraction notice or the fact-check, and to the exact place it is still used.

A case is not an accusation against the people who wrote the page. Most zombie facts are honest leftovers: the citation went in before the retraction, and nobody went back. Some pages cite a retracted paper on purpose, to discuss it. That's why a person reads every case before we contact anyone, and why some cases end as "reviewed, no fix needed".

Corrections policy

If we get something wrong, we fix it on the page, say what changed, and log it below with the date. We don't quietly delete mistakes. Errors in the data (a wrong date, a misread quote, a paper that isn't really retracted) are corrected as soon as we confirm them, and the daily build carries the fix to every page.

Contest a case

If you think a case is wrong, because the citation is there on purpose, the retraction doesn't touch the claim, or the source has been reinstated, tell us. Authors, editors and outlets are all welcome.

  1. Open the case page and press Contest this case. It opens a short form on GitHub, which is free and keeps every request and our answer public.
  2. Give us the evidence: a link to the reinstatement, the correction, or the sentence that discusses the retraction.
  3. We read it and reply on the same thread. If you're right, the case closes as "reviewed, no fix needed", the reason shows on the case page, and the fix goes in the log below.

Prefer Wikipedia? Reply under our note on the article's talk page. We read those replies every day.

Where the data comes from

  • English Wikipedia, read through its public API
  • Fact-checkers' own ratings (ClaimReview) via Data Commons
